Immigration

Statuses of Residence & Visas

Official visa categories under 2026 regulations. Review purposes, typical durations, and work allowances.

• Temporary Visitor: For tourism, family visits, or short business meetings. Valid for 15, 30, or up to 90 days. Work is strictly prohibited. Indians must apply in advance via visa processing centers (VFS).

• Transit Visa: For transit through airports. Valid up to 15 days. Work prohibited.

• Student Visa: For study at universities, vocational colleges, or language academies. Valid for 3 months to 2 years and 3 months. Work allowed up to 28 hours per week with a special stamp (Shikakugai Katsudo Kyoka).

• Cultural Activities: For unpaid internships, studying traditional arts, martial arts, or tea ceremonies. Valid for 3 months to 1 year. Work is prohibited.

• Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services (EHI): The most common professional work visa. Valid for 1, 3, or 5 years. Work is limited strictly to your contracted corporate field. Leads to PR track.

• Highly Skilled Professional (HSP): Points-based visa for elite profiles. Offers expedited Permanent Residency (PR in 3 years with 70 points, or 1 year with 80 points). Valid for 5 years.

• Business Manager: For startup founders or managing directors. Requires ¥5,000,000 capital (or ¥30,000,000 capital under new rules depending on corporate size) and a physical office space.

• Specified Skilled Worker (SSW): For trade industries (SSW1 valid up to 5 years, limited family sponsorship; SSW2 allows infinite renewals and family sponsorship).

• Spouse of Japanese National: Valid for 1, 3, or 5 years. Absolute work freedom (any industry, full-time allowed). Leads to PR in as little as 1 year of marriage + 3 years residency.

• Dependent Visa: For spouses and children of working residents. Valid based on parent's visa duration. Work allowed up to 28 hours per week with permission.

• Digital Nomad (Designated Activities): For remote workers. Valid up to 6 months (non-renewable). Requires ~¥10,000,000 annual income and private health insurance. Work for foreign entities allowed.

• J-Find (Designated Activities): For graduates of top-ranked worldwide universities looking for work in Japan. Valid up to 2 years.

• Permanent Resident (PR): Indefinite stay. Complete work freedom. Standard track is 10 years of continuous residence (including 5 years on a work visa), or faster via Highly Skilled points track.

• Naturalized Citizen: Acquisition of Japanese nationality. Requires 5 years residency, N3-N2 language capability, tax compliance, and relinquishing other passports.

Roadmap

The Relocation Journey

A typical chronological timeline from arrival to naturalization.

Step 1: Entry & Temporary Setup

Month 1

Arrival, border control checks, receiving the Residence Card (Zairyu Card) at the airport, and registering your physical address at the municipal office.

Step 2: Integration & Acculturation

Years 1–3

Mastering local garbage segregation rules, gaining conversational proficiency (JLPT N4–N3), enrolling in municipal tax systems, and establishing credit history.

Step 3: Professional Progression

Years 3–5

Visa renewals, career moves, switching to self-employment, and upgrading to the Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) points-based track.

Step 4: Permanent Settlement (PR)

Year 10 (or Year 1–3 via HSP)

Applying for Permanent Residency. Requires continuous tax and pension records, clean legal background, and solid stability in Japan.

Regulations

Rules, Laws & Deportations

Understanding legal duties, local rules, and offenses leading to visa cancellation.

• Address changes: You must update your address at the local ward office within 14 days of moving. Failure to notify can freeze administrative renewals.

• Worldwide Income Tax: After residing in Japan for a cumulative 5 years, you qualify as a "permanent tax resident" and Japan can tax your income sourced globally.

• Re-entry Permits: If departing Japan for more than 1 year, you must apply for a re-entry permit. Under 1 year, you receive a special automatic re-entry permit at airport immigration.

• Zero Tolerance Drunk Driving: The legal blood-alcohol limit is 0.00%. Driving or cycling under the influence of alcohol leads to immediate arrest, massive fines, and visa cancellation.

• Bicycle laws: Police perform regular random checks on bicycle registration to prevent theft. Cycling on sidewalks is restricted, and using umbrellas or headphones while cycling is fined.

• Noise Curfews: Vacuuming, doing laundry, or playing music after 9-10 PM is considered extremely rude due to thin apartment walls.

• Visa Overstay: Staying even one day past your visa expiration date without an active extension application is a serious offense leading to detention and deportation.

• Unauthorized Work: Working even a single day on a tourist visa or exceeding the 28-hour student limit will lead to deportations and host employer penalties.

• Convictions: Japan maintains a 99% conviction rate. Any arrest for theft, physical disputes, or drug violations results in career termination and immediate deportation.

• Tax Evasion: Intentionally refusing to pay income tax or resident tax for multiple consecutive years will lead to visa rejection or deportation.

Language

Language Integration

Understanding JLPT levels, Japanese scripts, and translation hacks.

The 3 Scripts

1. Hiragana: Basic phonetic alphabet for native Japanese words (46 characters).

2. Katakana: Phonetic alphabet specifically used for foreign loan words, names, and menu items (46 characters).

3. Kanji: Chinese characters representing full ideas or words. You need about 2,000 to read a daily newspaper.

JLPT Proficiency Levels

• N5 & N4: Basic phrases, travel-level conversations, hiragana/katakana capability.

• N3: Conversational level. Essential for daily life independence and basic service jobs.

• N2 & N1: Professional level. N2 is the absolute minimum requirement for office jobs, engineering desks, and visa fast-tracks.

Camera Mode Translation Tip

Since electricity bills, municipal notices, and postal receipts are 100% in Japanese, install Google Translate. Its camera mode translates any Japanese text to English in real time, preventing late fees or legal oversights.

Medical

Universal Healthcare & Welfare

How health insurance works, and vital warnings regarding imported medications.

National Health Insurance

Japan operates on a 70/30 payment split. The government covers 70% of medical and dental expenses; you pay a 30% co-pay out of pocket. Walk-in clinic visits are cheap and fast.

Clinic-First Referral System

Do not visit big hospitals first. The Japanese healthcare system requires you to visit a local clinic first. If needed, the clinic doctor writes a referral letter. Walking into a hospital without a referral incurs a high surcharge.

⚠️ Banned Prescription Medicines Warning

Many common Indian prescription medications (including certain painkillers, strong cough syrups, ADHD medications, and sleeping pills) contain active ingredients that are classified as narcotics or illegal stimulants under Japanese law. Carrying them into the country without prior government approval (Yakkan Shoumei) leads to immediate customs confiscation and arrest at the border.

Career

Workplace Culture Deep-Dive

Understanding key consensus models, formal hierarchies, and social expectations.

Nemawashi

"Pruning the roots." Before any official meeting, managers consult all affected parties individually to reach consensus. Official decisions are rarely debated in public meetings.

Ringi System

A bottom-up decision-making model where proposals circulate on a document to receive approval stamps (hanko) from every tier of management before execution.

Nomikai

After-work drinking parties with colleagues. While technically optional, attendance is culturally expected to build workplace trusts (Nomication) and network.

Paid Leave & Workplace Reality

Paid leave is a legal right protected by Japanese labor laws. However, culturally, taking all of your holiday can be frowned upon as putting extra burden on team members. Most corporate workers take only half their entitlement. Navigate this diplomatically, and ensure your N2/N1 Keigo is practiced when raising concerns privately.

Indians in Japan

Special Guide for Indians

Critical tips, dietary workarounds, community networks, and sending money home.

🥘 Vegetarian & Vegan Options Difficulty

Vegetarianism is not widely understood in Japan. Even dishes marked "vegetable" or "tofu" almost always contain fish stock (dashi) or pork extract. When dining, ask specifically: "Dashi wa sakana desu ka?" (Is the broth fish-based?). Seek Indian vegetarian dining in Tokyo's Nishi-Kasai area (known locally as "Little India").

💸 International Money Transfers

Avoid using traditional Japanese commercial banks for sending money back to India — they are slow, expensive, and require tedious paperwork. Services like Wise or Revolut offer immediate conversions and much cheaper rates. Set them up using your Residence Card.

👥 Community Networks

Connect with active Indian community networks on Facebook and WhatsApp. Major temples, such as the ISKCON temple in Tokyo, serve as excellent social networks offering guidance on housing, visas, and employment referrals.

Seasons & Weather You Must Know

Evaluating seasonal shifts, humidity, and national holiday clusters.

Season & Months Climatic Conditions Key Events & Evacuation Notes
Spring (Mar – May) Mild, pleasant weather. cherry blossoms bloom. Golden Week (late April – early May): Entire country travels simultaneously. Hotel rates triple.
Rainy Season (Tsuyu) (June – July) High humidity, constant damp rain. Mold prevention is critical indoors. Use dehumidifiers.
Summer (July – August) Extreme heat and humidity (up to 38°C, 80%+ humidity). Higher humidity than Bihar/Delhi. Stock up on hydration. O-Bon festival (mid-August).
Typhoon Season (August – October) Severe wind, heavy rain, public transit halts. Typhoons strike regularly. Check evacuation routes. Download NHK World app.
Winter (Dec – Feb) Cold, crisp, heavy snow in northern areas (Hokkaido). New Year (Oshogatsu): Major shutdown from Jan 1–3. Stock up on groceries.
Safety

Emergency & Public Safety

Dialing rescue services, surviving earthquakes, and understanding train etiquettes.

📞 Emergency Contact Lines

• Police (110): Dial for traffic accidents, reporting theft, or physical security emergencies.

• Ambulance & Fire (119): Dial for injuries, fire outbreaks, or immediate health emergencies. Free from any phone; translation options available.

🌋 Earthquake Survival

Japan sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire. Learn the basic "Drop, Cover, Hold" safety drill. Evacuation maps are posted in every hotel room and apartment corridor. Check local municipality maps for regional evacuation parks.

Train Safety & Chikan (Groping)

Crowded transit trains in major metropolitans face issues with chikan (groping). To protect passengers, railway systems designate specific "Women Only" carriages during morning and evening rush hours. Respect these rules, and always keep your hands visible (holding phone or bags) in packed subway trains.

Finance

Money, Banking & Tax Deductions

Maximizing cashless points and navigating local tax breaks.

Rakuten & PayPay Point Culture

Cashless points save real money. Set up Rakuten Card or PayPay early. Almost all convenience stores, restaurants, and shopping malls offer loyalty points that can be redeemed directly at the cash counter.

Furusato Nozei Hometown Tax

A highly popular tax deduction system. You donate a portion of your local resident tax to rural towns. In return, the towns send you local goods (beef, rice, fruits) and the donation is deducted from your next year's taxes.

Real Estate

Renting & Housing Etiquette

Understanding upfront lease fees, guarantor systems, and apartment manners.

Key Upfront Renting Fees

• Key Money (Reikin): A non-refundable "gift" to the landlord, usually 1–2 months of rent. Becoming less common but still standard in traditional agencies.

• Deposit (Shikikin): 1–2 months of rent. Refunded upon moving out, minus cleaning fees and repair costs for any damage (including tatami damage).

Guarantor System

Traditional leases require a Japanese joint guarantor (Hoshonin). For foreign residents, specialized guarantor companies (like GTN or agency-approved ones) act as guarantors for a fee (typically 50-100% of a month's rent).

Tatami Straw-Mat Rules

Traditional tatami rooms require strict manners. Never walk on tatami with shoes or slippers (bare feet or socks only). Do not place heavy, sharp furniture directly on the mats. If tatami is punctured or stained, you will pay full replacement costs upon moving out.

Education

School Education for Families

Navigating public vs international schools and local classroom customs.

Public Schooling

Free public schooling is guaranteed for all children of foreign residents. Classes are taught 100% in Japanese. English support is rare, but young children typically adapt and become fluent within 1–2 years.

International Schools

Located in major cities (Tokyo, Yokohama, Osaka). Offer English/IB curriculums but are expensive, costing between ¥1.5 million to ¥3.0 million annually per child.

Classroom Cleaning Culture (O-Soji)

Japanese public schools do not hire janitors. Students themselves clean the classrooms, corridors, and even serve lunch to each other daily. This is a core part of building community spirit and personal responsibility from a young age.

Animals

Pet Importation Rules

The strict biological clearance steps required to bring dogs or cats to Japan.

Pet Import Clearance Timeline

1. Microchip: Implant a ISO 11784/11785 compliant microchip first (before rabies vaccination).

2. Dual Rabies Shots: Give at least two inactivated rabies vaccinations spaced appropriately.

3. Rabies Titre Test: Draw blood for a rabies antibody test and send to a government-approved lab.

4. 180-Day Wait: A mandatory 180-day quarantine wait begins from the blood draw date. The pet can stay in the origin country during this time.

5. Import Notification: File notification of import to Japan Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) at least 40 days before departure.

⚠️ Critical Note: Start this process at least 1 year in advance. Failing any step results in 180-day airport detention at your expense.

Transit

Driving & License Conversion

Converting foreign licenses to a Japanese driver's license.

Foreign License Conversion (Gaimen Kirikae)

If you hold a valid Indian driver's license, you can convert it at a prefecture driving center. The conversion requires:

• Proof of residing in India for at least 90 days after license issuance.

• Official translation of the license from Japan Automobile Federation (JAF).

• Passing a brief written exam and a practical driving test at the center.

⚠️ Test Tip: The practical road test has a low first-time pass rate for Indians due to strict lane-checking rules. Study the course map and practice turning angles beforehand.
